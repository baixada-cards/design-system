# 04 — Voice & Tone

How Baixada sounds.

## The two voices

**Editorial voice.** Calm, lettered, slightly antique. Used on marketing pages, About, the lab, and any surface where we have the user's full attention. It is the voice of someone who has thought about cards for a long time and is now writing it down.

**Warm voice.** Hand-written, first-person, particular. Used in achievements, marginalia, splash hand-offs. It is the voice of a relative who plays the game, not a brand.

The two voices share the same vocabulary. They differ in posture: editorial sits up; warm leans on the table.

## House rules

1. **No exclamation marks.** Anywhere. The brand never shouts.
2. **No emoji** in product copy. (Emoji are fine in a casual social-media reply, never in a UI string.)
3. **Sentence case** for every UI label, button, and heading except plaques and kickers.
4. **No corporate game-app idioms.** Forbidden phrases include: *level up*, *unlock*, *crush it*, *epic*, *legendary*, *daily streak*, *play to win*, *grind*. The game *is* a grind sometimes; we don't say so.
5. **The Oxford comma is mandatory** in English. In Portuguese, follow standard usage.
6. **Numbers under ten are spelled out** in editorial; numerals everywhere else.

## Language conventions

English, Brazilian Portuguese, and Spanish are first-class. Strings are
translated, not paraphrased.

- **Title-case English headings** become **sentence-case Portuguese and Spanish headings** following their conventions (only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized).
- **Quotation marks**: `"…"` in English; `« … »` or `"…"` in Portuguese — pick one and stay consistent.
- **Dates**: ISO short form (`2026-04-28`) in technical contexts; long form locale-correct in editorial.
- **The brand name "Baixada"** is **never translated**. Not in English, not in Spanish, not in Italian.
- **Game names "Truco", "Escopa", "Bisca"** are **never translated**. Not even to "Italian Scopa".
- **The `· Lab` suffix** is the same in all languages. We do not localize it to "Laboratório" or "Laboratorio".

## Where the warm voice lives

- **Achievement titles** (`o Truco do Vô João`).
- **Empty-state messages** (one or two per app — the moments when the user has just sat down).
- **Easter-egg footer signatures**.
- **Match-result hand-written annotations** when an unusual hand is played.

## Where the warm voice does not live

- Buttons.
- Form labels.
- Error messages (errors are precise and editorial, never cute).
- Settings.
- Onboarding flow.
- The lab — the lab is editorial only.

**Homepage hero, EN.**

**Homepage hero, PT-BR.**

**Homepage hero, ES.**

**Lab section heading, EN.**
> Equilibria, hand by hand.

**Lab section heading, PT.**
> Equilíbrios, mão por mão.

**Empty state, table waiting for an opponent, EN.**
> Pull up a chair. The deck is shuffled.

**Empty state, PT.**
> Puxa uma cadeira. O baralho está pronto.

**Achievement, warm voice.**
> *o Truco do Vô João* — won a hand on a 12-point bluff with 4-5-6.

**Error message, editorial.**
> The match could not be saved. Your hand is intact; please try again.

## Things to avoid in copy

- Brazilian-Portuguese gauchesque vocabulary (*tchê*, *guri*, *bah*) outside of explicit Easter-eggs. Even the warm voice avoids regional caricature.
- Italian sprinkles (*nonno*, *famiglia*) — the family is Italian-descended, but the brand voice is Portuguese-and-English. Italian only appears where it would in the family's actual conversation: rare, specific.
- Anglicisms in PT copy that have a clean Portuguese equivalent (write *partida*, not *match*).
- Lusitanisms in PT-BR copy (write *você*, not *tu*; *ônibus* not *autocarro* — but this product is unlikely to need either).
