---

## The umbrella: Baixada

**Why this name.**

- It is **specific**. *Baixada* is a Portuguese proper noun with a real,
  personal association for the author. That association does not need to be
  explained in public-facing copy.
- It survives the **portfolio test**. Specific Portuguese nouns ("São Paulo", "Açaí", "Saudade") read as evocative to non-Brazilians, not awkward. We do not need the name to be transparent in English.
- It is **flexible**. It scales from a single-game launch to a multi-game home without renaming.

**Pronunciation guide for non-Brazilian readers.**

> *bye-SHAH-dah* — `/baɪˈʃadɐ/`. Three syllables, stress on the middle.

The brand should never apologize for the pronunciation. A small phonetic gloss
in About pages is fine; do not force it into the wordmark or attach an origin
story to it.

**What "Baixada" means in copy.**

- *the home page* — the umbrella site itself
- *the family of products* — "Baixada is shipping Truco first."

It is **not** a generic word for "card games" or "lobby". When you mean the lobby, write "the lobby". When you mean Baixada, write Baixada.

---

## Tagline

> **EN — card games and optimal play**
> **PT-BR — jogos de carta e estratégia ótima**
> **ES — juegos de cartas y estrategia óptima**

The tagline does two things:

1. Says what the products contain: card games.
2. Names the distinctive addition: tools for studying optimal play.

It deliberately makes no geographic-origin claim about the games or the
project. Baixada has a personal history; that history is not a substitute for
the actual origins of Truco, Escopa, or Bisca, nor for where the software is
being made.

**Use the tagline only on the umbrella surface** — homepage, social profiles, About, app icons that lock to the umbrella mark. Individual games do **not** carry the tagline; they carry their own subtitles if needed.

**Do not translate it loosely.** English, Brazilian Portuguese, and Spanish
are canonical above. If another language is needed, get it translated by a
native speaker; do not paraphrase.

## The games

The games keep their **real names**. We do not invent new names for Truco, Escopa, or Bisca. Doing so would be like renaming Poker.

- **Truco** — the variant is *truco paulista*, 1v1. The product can clarify in About or settings; the brand says only "Truco".
- **Escopa** — full name *Escopa de Quinze* (Escopa de 15). The product surface uses "Escopa" in the wordmark; the variant is named in About / rules.
- **Bisca** — same. Wordmark "Bisca", details elsewhere.

### The `· Lab` suffix

The `· Lab` suffix marks a game variant that ships with a **CFR-solved opponent** and a study surface for inspecting equilibria.

- The mid-dot is **U+00B7** (`·`), not a hyphen, em dash, or bullet. It must be surrounded by spaces.
- Always written **after** the game name: `Truco · Lab`. Never `Lab · Truco`.
- A game may ship in two registers: **Truco** (casual play) and **Truco · Lab** (study). They are the same product internally; the suffix marks the mode.
- If Escopa or Bisca eventually get solvers, the same suffix applies (`Escopa · Lab`). Until then, do not preemptively use it.

## Combined names — when to put Baixada first vs. last

**Web (umbrella-first):** `Baixada · Truco`, `Baixada · Escopa`. The user is browsing the umbrella site; the game is the room.

**App stores / install surfaces (game-first):** `Truco · Baixada`, `Escopa · Baixada`. App-store search prioritizes leading words; the game name has to lead so generic searches for "Truco" rank well.

**Inside an app:** just the game name. `Truco`. The umbrella shows up only in the About screen and the splash.

---

## Naming rules going forward

A short rulebook for any future product, partnership, or microsite touching the brand.

1. **Real game names always win.** If a real card game has a real name, we use it verbatim. We do not invent.
2. **Suffixes describe modes, not flavors.** `· Lab` means a solver is present. We do not invent suffixes for marketing colors ("Truco · Pro" is forbidden).
3. **One umbrella name.** We do not invent sub-brands. Everything is `Baixada · X` or `X · Baixada` with the existing inventory.
4. **Trilingual equality.** English, Brazilian Portuguese, and Spanish are
   first-class. Game and brand names remain unchanged across them: "Truco"
   never becomes "Trick", and "Escopa" never becomes "Scopa" even though
   Italian Scopa is the cognate ancestor.
5. **No emoji in product names.** Ever.
6. **No version numbers in user-facing names.** "Truco · Lab v2" is a build number, not a name. Users see "Truco · Lab".

## Names we considered and rejected

Recorded here so we don't relitigate them.

- **Sobremesa** — beautiful, evocative, works PT/EN. Rejected because it suggests post-meal lingering, not active play. Could become a future product (a quiet end-of-night replay viewer?).
- **A Baixada** — the article is more place-like, but the leading article makes English sentences awkward ("A Baixada is shipping…"). Bare *Baixada* won.
- **The Long Table / Card Table / Mesa** — translated cousins. Rejected for being generic. *Baixada* is more specific and therefore more memorable.
- **Família / Reunião** — too sentimental; loses the "study/lab" register entirely.
- **Baixadinha / Baixadão** — diminutives that are charming in Portuguese but unintelligible to non-speakers.
- **Generic "GameStudio" portmanteaus** — rejected by default. We are not that kind of project.
